TL;DR

  • The primary user is the "Symptom-Solver": someone seeking practical tools for executive function challenges, regardless of a clinical diagnosis.

  • There are two primary archetypes who fall under this umbrella: the Aspiring Achiever (student/young professional) and the Proactive Life Organizer (adult/parent).

  • A key secondary archetype is the Quietly Coping Neurodivergent, who requires discreet but powerful support.

1. The "Symptom-Solver" Framework: The Unifying Principle

The "Symptom-Solver" is the unifying framework used to understand the entire user base. It represents a strategic shift away from a narrow, clinical focus and toward a broad, benefit-driven narrative of self-improvement. This model is a direct response to the evidence that the need for Myndmap's solutions is far broader than the formally diagnosed ADHD population.

The data is unambiguous: Based on the data collected during beta, a combined 61.7% of the user base either explicitly states they do not have ADHD (6.4%) or falls into the "Unknown/Missing" category (55.3%). These are not passive or unengaged users; they are actively downloading an executive function support app and reporting the same core struggles with "Time Management," "Organization," and "Task Initiation" as diagnosed users.  

This behavior cannot be understood without considering the Japanese cultural context. A powerful stigma surrounds mental health diagnoses, with reports indicating that a vast majority of people would feel uneasy consulting a psychiatrist for fear of negative social or career repercussions. Consequently, the choice to remain "Unknown" is likely a deliberate one. These users are seeking help for a symptom (e.g., "I am forgetful," "I procrastinate") rather than adopting a clinical identity (e.g., "I have ADHD").  

2. Primary Archetype 1: The Aspiring Achiever

Who they are: This segment is composed of individuals aged 18-34, encompassing university students and early-career professionals. They form the core of the current active user base and are the engine of initial adoption. As digital natives, they are comfortable with technology and are proactively seeking solutions to manage the immense pressure to succeed in Japan's demanding academic and corporate environments.  

Core Motivations: Their engagement with Myndmap is instrumental and goal-oriented, driven by powerful academic and career ambitions. They use the app not just for daily organization, but as a critical tool for managing high-stakes, future-defining projects.

Behavioral Evidence: Their logged reminders and tasks are a direct testament to their ambitions. The data is replete with complex, multi-stage projects that carry significant cognitive load and real-world consequences.

  • Academic Pursuits: "研究計画書 骨組みつくる" (create research plan framework), "論文のまとめ" (summarize thesis), "民訴22" (Civil Procedure 22), and "卒論" (graduation thesis) are common entries, reflecting the pressures of higher education.  

  • Career Progression: Tasks such as "応募書類作成" (prepare application documents) and "企画書" (proposal) demonstrate the app's role in navigating the job market and early professional responsibilities.  

Pain Points: Their most frequently reported challenges are "Time Management" and "Task Initiation". They often feel overwhelmed by the sheer volume and ambiguity of their projects. This leads to a state of paralysis and procrastination, where the difficulty of starting a complex task becomes a significant barrier to progress.  

JP/EN Nuance: The pressures faced by this archetype are uniquely amplified in the Japanese context. The rigorous university examination system and the highly structured "new graduate" job-hunting process (shūkatsu) create an intense, culturally specific need for sophisticated self-management and organizational tools. The app serves as a crucial support system for navigating these high-pressure life stages.

This defines the total addressable market not by a diagnosis, but by a shared set of problems. The market consists of anyone in Japan feeling overwhelmed by the demands of modern life on their executive function a much larger and more commercially viable segment. Adopting the "Symptom-Solver" framework allows for the development of features and messaging that resonate with the broadest possible audience. It enables the use of culturally positive and destigmatized language centered on universally valued concepts like self-improvement (kaizen), efficiency (効率化), and organization (整理).

3. Primary Archetype 2: The Proactive Life Organizer

Who they are: This profile represents adults, typically aged 25-45+, who are managing the intricate web of professional work, family responsibilities, and personal life administration. They are motivated by a desire for stability, reliability, and a reduction of the constant mental load associated with modern adulthood.  

Core Motivations: Their primary goal is to create a sense of order and control, preventing critical tasks and information from "falling through the cracks." They are not necessarily striving for peak performance in a single domain but are seeking a holistic system to manage the complexity of their entire life.

Behavioral Evidence: Their usage patterns center on comprehensive life administration and the organization of their physical environment.

  • Life Administration: They log reminders for recurring responsibilities like "家賃支払い6万" (rent payment 60,000) and crucial family coordination tasks such as managing the "連絡帳" (a communication book between parents and school teachers).  

  • Household Management: A significant number of tasks relate to daily chores, such as "洗濯干す" (hang laundry) and "部屋の片付け" (tidy up room), indicating a need to structure and manage the domestic sphere.  

Pain Points: Their dominant challenge is "Organization". They struggle with the cognitive burden of remembering countless appointments, recurring bills, school events, and the needs of multiple family members. This leads to a constant, low-level anxiety about forgetting something important.  

A defining behavior of this archetype is the creation of a "digital twin" of their physical world to outsource cognition. The organizationAreas data is filled with hyper-specific physical locations like "ベージュワゴン" (beige wagon), "本棚左側" (left side of bookshelf), and "台所の出窓" (kitchen bay window). This is not arbitrary labeling; it is a deliberate cognitive strategy to map their digital organizational system directly onto their physical reality. They are building an external brain to remember what needs to be done where.

4. Secondary Archetype 3: The Quietly Coping Neurodivergent

Who they are: This persona includes any adult with a formal diagnosis of ADHD or a strong, well-researched suspicion of it. They are consciously and actively managing their symptoms in environments that may lack understanding or accommodation.

Core Motivations: Their primary driver is the need to function effectively and meet the high expectations of neurotypical academic and professional settings without having to disclose their condition. Due to the significant social stigma surrounding ADHD and mental health in Japan, privacy and discretion are paramount. They seek empowerment through tools that feel like productivity enhancers, not medical aids.  

Pain Points: This archetype experiences all the executive function challenges of the other personas but with the added emotional and cognitive burden of masking their difficulties. They are more likely to select the broad term "Executive Function" as their primary challenge, signifying a cluster of related difficulties with planning, working memory, and emotional regulation. They fear judgment and misunderstanding, and they need tools that are intuitive and adaptable to their unique ways of thinking.  

What They Need from Myndmap: The app must function as a "private co-pilot". The user experience must be calming and non-judgmental, adhering to the company's "Goldilocks Principles" of providing just-right difficulty and visible progress. Features must be designed to support neurodivergent thinking patterns, avoiding overwhelming clutter and providing clear, customizable feedback loops. For this user, the ability to personalize the sensory experience (e.g., themes, notification sounds) is not a cosmetic feature but a core component of usability and comfort.
